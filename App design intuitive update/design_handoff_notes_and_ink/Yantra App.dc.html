07 · A TASK'S PAGE IS A PAGE
Every task opens a document of blocks — prose, headings, bullets, numbered lines, ink, images, and other tasks — each with an indent. So the page is not a form with a note field on it. It is an outline, and a subtask is just a line that happens to have a page of its own.
One column, one indent scale. 20dp per level, one hairline per block, no cards. A page of eleven blocks has to stay quiet.
Blocks carry no chrome at rest. No drag handles, no per-block menus, no type badges. The block's own shape says what it is.
Nested tasks keep the full row grammar — glyph, ladder, trailing slot — because they are tasks. Drag one right on this page and it starts, same gesture as the day list.
Ink and images are blocks, not attachments. They sit in the flow where you put them, at the width of the column, and open full-bleed on tap.
One insert affordance at the end of the page and on Enter. Six kinds, no submenus.

08 · IN PROGRESS, END TO END
In progress is a status in the data, so it should be a status in the flow: not a badge you notice, but the reason the app looks different while it is true. Five places carry it, and they are the same five on every screen.
1 · The glyph. Drawn ring — the only row in the list wearing one.
2 · The row's ground. A coral wash, the only tinted row on the screen.
3 · The trailing slot shows elapsed time in coral instead of a deadline. Effort outranks schedule while the clock is on.
4 · Position. It leaves its section and pins to the top of whatever list you are looking at, so it is never scrolled away from.
5 · The now bar replaces the capture bar app-wide until the session ends. Capture moves into the leading key of the tab strip and stays reachable.
Exactly one task can be running. That is what makes the treatment legible — a wash means nothing if three rows have it. Starting a second offers SWITCH HERE, never a second clock.

09 · TABLET · THE TASK'S PAGE
Same two panes as the ink screen, different right-hand content: the page itself, with its ink blocks live in the flow. You write into a block where it sits rather than opening it — that is the whole reason the tablet exists.
The column stays a column. 720dp of measure centred in the pane, not stretched to the glass — a page of prose at 1300dp wide is unreadable.
Ink blocks are full-column and always live. Pen down anywhere inside one and it draws; palm and finger scroll. No mode to enter.
The puck only appears once the pen is near a block, and it sits at the writing hand's side of the canvas.
Nested tasks keep the ladder here too, so a page can be worked as a checklist without leaving it.
Properties are chips under the title — the same chips as the phone, one row, no sidebar of fields.

10 · FULL-SCREEN INK
One ink block, the whole glass. The layout follows what the established handwriting apps converge on: nobody browses a tool library mid-sentence, they keep two or three pens they already trust and switch between them without looking.
A pen kit, not a palette. Three slots — fine, bold, marker — each drawn as the stroke it actually makes, so you pick by sight and one tap. Hold a slot to change what it holds; that is the only place width and colour live.
The eraser is a gesture, not a trip. Double-tap the pen, or hold its button, and the active slot flips to erase until you lift. This is the single biggest speed difference between the apps that feel good and the ones that don't.
Chrome dims while you write and never moves, so the kit is where you left it. Undo and redo stay at full strength in the far corner.
Selection is where the app earns its keep. Lasso, then the ink becomes something: text, or a task on this page. Every other app stops at "copy".
There are no pages. The surface scrolls down for as long as you keep writing and grows a screenful ahead of the pen, so the bottom edge is never a wall you have to do something about. Nothing to add, nothing to number, no page chrome at all.
Position is a scrollbar, briefly. A hairline appears at the left edge while you scroll and leaves when you stop — the only indicator a continuous surface needs.
Pen draws, finger scrolls, two fingers zoom. Pressure and tilt vary the stroke; the palm is ignored. There is no mode to be in the wrong one of.
